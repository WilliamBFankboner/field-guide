MARSHALL MCLUHAN

Understanding Media

Influences

"I am pleased to think of my own book The Gutenberg Galaxy as a footnote to the observations of Innis on the subject of the psychic and social consequences, first of writing then of printing." --Marshall McLuhan

"Harold Innis was the first person to hit upon the process of change as implicit in the forms of media technology. The present book is a footnote of explanation to his work." --Marshall McLuhan

In the first introduction to Understanding Media, McLuhan mentions the 'consternation of one of the editors':

He noted in dismay that "seventy-five per cent of your material is new. A successful book cannot venture to be more than ten per cent new."

Where did all this 'new' material come from? It is apparent from the final sentence of The Gutenberg Galaxy that McLuhan considered it and Understanding Media to be companions pieces, parts of a two-volume work, rather than as separate standalone works. Most of McLuhan's ideas can be traced to authorities he acknowledged and quoted in The Gutenberg Galaxy., which he called 'a useful prelude to 'the rewrite of Understanding Media.'

This is not to say these concepts had not already been percolating in McLuhan's mind prior to his encounter with these scholars: the history of ideas is rife with mutual discovery and most intellectual breakthroughs have been fathered independently and simultaneously by more than one thinker. Or as William Saroyan put it, "The greatest performances are never seen, and never even suspected as actual events."

In fact, McLuhan was generous about his influences and too self-confident to quibble about who got there first. In fact he said of William Ong... Of Harold Innis he said... One get the feeling that he would have been right at home in Medieval times before the notion of copyright became waxed so important.

Whether McLuhan cited these autorities as proof of percptions he had developed on his own is unknown, but McLuhan was not one to quibble over who came first; he was confident enough of his own contribution: the unprecedented synthesis of  these ideas, his ability to put so many of these ideas in play in pursuit of a metaphysical vision.

What then is so original about Understanding Media? It is fair to say McLuhan saw signficance (and connections) in the works of other authors who, understandably, did not appreciate or pursue the metaphysical implications of their own discoveries. Understanding Media is a brilliant synthesis of Ong, Freud, Toynbee, Lewis Mumford, Gyory Kepes, and a host of other scholars:

The origin of the alphabet + print = space, i.e. man's visual bias, is due to Father Ong, to which McLuhan dedicates his works as a foonote (p.192-3, GG). This is not a symolic gesture or a selfless act of modety, but a simple acknowledgement of fact. In terms of  scholarly debt, it's as if UM is the second volume of GG, in which.MM has already credited Ong as the originator of his prmary thesis.

McLuhan frequently quoted Walter Ong's Ramus, Method, and the Decay of Dialogue (1958), which evidently had prompted McLuhan to write The Gutenberg Galaxy. Ong wrote a highly favorable review of this new book in America.[28] However, Ong later tempered his praise, by describing McLuhan's The Gutenberg Galaxy as "a racy survey, indifferent to some scholarly detail, but uniquely valuable in suggesting the sweep and depth of the cultural and psychological changes entailed in the passage from illiteracy to print and beyond."[29] McLuhan himself said of the book, "I'm not concerned to get any kudos out of [The Gutenberg Galaxy]. It seems to me a book that somebody should have written a century ago. I wish somebody else had written it. It will be a useful prelude to the rewrite of Understanding Media [the 1960 NAEB report] that I'm doing now."[citation needed]
