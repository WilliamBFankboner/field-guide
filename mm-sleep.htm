Sleep as a Metaphor: Levels of Inattention

Consciousness, complex and subtle, can be impaired or ended by a mere stepping-up or dimming-down of any one sense intensity, which is the procedure in hypnosis. And the intensification of one sense by a new medium can hypnotize an entire community. ("Numbers" Chaper 11)

The less sentient levels of human consciousness are described by McLuhan as varying degrees of mental alertness. E.g. subrational trance, typographic trance, swoon, hypnogogia, somnambulism, narcosis, automatic behavior, psychic rigor mortis, fragmentation, dissociation, specialization and linear perspective, are all stages of oblivion in the continuum of sleep. Thus, any condition that diminishes man's capacity to achieve spirituality, in-depth awareness, or higher states of consciousness is considered a kind of sleep; gathering wool or day-dreaming are forms of inattention or unawareness that can cause one to nod off and enter a dream state, i.e. actual physiological sleep; but note that a person who is too literal-minded, or incapable of thinking in metaphorical terms, or a person whose knowledge is specialized, is also in some sense 'asleep.'

Because it denudes religion of its transcendental essence, Blake regarded rationalism ('single vision') as a form of sleep. Higher spiritual levels are described in terms of integral or inclusive awareness, the ability to perceive configuration and to see things in terms of other things, e.g. metaphorically (analogical, metaphorical and symbolic awareness); the highest kind of awareness being metaphysical understanding and the apprehension of universal truth.
